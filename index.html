The Gap On Campus

A full card next to an empty tray

Right now, sharing a spare swipe means posting in a group chat, hoping someone sees it in time, then meeting up in person at a dining hall before it expires. It works sometimes. Most semesters, plenty of swipes just expire unused instead.

10 PERCENT

About 10% of Georgia Tech students reported needing food assistance in a 2016 campus survey. THAT'S A LOT OF YELLOW JACKETS SKIPPING MEALS ON A CAMPUS WHERE SWIPES GO TO WASTE.

MealShare exists to connect those two facts before another semester passes, by building the exchange directly into the system students already use every day.

The Process

Two tickets, one pool

A donor's ticket and a recipient's ticket both end at the same place: a shared pool of swipes that Georgia Tech Dining manages behind the scenes.

For Donors

Give a swipe

  1. Log in with your GT single sign-on. Your BuzzCard is already linked, nothing new to set up.

  2. See your remaining balance. Your dashboard shows exactly how many swipes are left this semester.

  3. Choose how many to donate. Send any number of unused swipes into the shared pool.

YOUR SWIPE JOINS THE POOL. GT DINING TAKES IT FROM THERE.

For Recipients

Request a swipe

  1. Apply through a separate, confidential portal. It is not linked to the donor side of MealShare.

  2. Your eligibility is verified privately. The Dean of Students office handles the review, not other students.

  3. Approved swipes land on your BuzzCard. They work like any other swipe at any dining hall.

YOUR BUZZCARD WORKS AS NORMAL. NO ONE AT THE REGISTER CAN TELL.

Backed By

Dignity by design

At the register, a donated swipe and a regular swipe look exactly the same. No special line, no separate card, and no way for a cashier, a friend, or anyone else to tell the difference.

Questions

What students usually ask

Straight answers to the questions that matter most before you donate or apply.

Will anyone know I donated or requested a swipe?

No. Donors and recipients never have to interact, and you never have to explain your situation to anyone. Because donated and regular swipes look identical at the point of sale, there is nothing to reveal at checkout either.

Am I eligible to request swipes?

Eligibility is verified through a confidential needs assessment run by the Dean of Students office, not by fellow students or dining staff. The recipient portal is separate from the donor side of MealShare.

Does my donated swipe go to a specific person?

Your swipe goes into a shared pool managed through the Green Forks and Georgia Tech Dining partnership. Approved recipients draw from that pool, which is what keeps the process anonymous on both sides.

How do I actually donate?

Log in with your GT single sign-on, which connects to your BuzzCard automatically. From there you can see your remaining balance and choose how many swipes to send into the shared pool.

Is MealShare an official Georgia Tech program?

MealShare is a proposed initiative built through a partnership between Green Forks, a Georgia Tech student organization, and Georgia Tech Dining, in support of GT's Strategic Plan and the UN's Zero Hunger goal.

Ready to close the gap?

Whether you have swipes to spare or need one this week, MealShare makes it simple and private. No stigma, no awkward group chat, just your BuzzCard.
